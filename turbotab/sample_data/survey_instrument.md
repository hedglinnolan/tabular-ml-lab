# `survey_instrument.csv` — expected findings

**300 rows × 45 columns.** One 40-item instrument, five-point Likert.
`respondent_id`, `age`, `sex`, `education`, `item_01`…`item_40`, and the outcome
`sought_support` (binary).

Generated by `make_fixtures.py::survey`, seed `1177`.

---

## The shape, measured

| Property | Value | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| Response levels | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | ordinal because the **instrument** says so |
| Reverse-coded items | 8: `item_05`, `11`, `17`, `23`, `29`, `34`, `38`, `40` | the codebook's fact, not the data's |
| Mean corr(reverse item, forward-item mean) | **−0.56** | negative, and that is the trap |
| Mean corr(forward item, forward-item mean) | +0.55 | for contrast |
| Blanks | 12 each in `item_07`, `item_19`, `item_31` | skipped items, which is what a real return looks like |
| Outcome | external, not a scale total | a total built from the items would leak through every feature |

---

## Must surface

1. **Ordinal encoding is declared, never frequency-derived.** The order comes
   from the instrument, which makes the encoding **row-local** — it executes
   immediately under constitution §06's litmus, because the mapping for row *i*
   depends on nothing but row *i*. An encoding learned from the observed
   frequencies would be a statistical transform and would have to be deferred;
   getting this wrong misclassifies the whole step.
2. **Reverse-coding is asked.** Never inferred. The app states which items it
   would need told about and offers the multi-select.
3. **The 40 items are one instrument**, not 40 unrelated features and not one
   feature measured 40 times.

---

## Must NOT surface

- **No reverse-coding inferred from the item correlations.** This is the sharpest
  *must not* in the whole fixture set, and the fixture is built to make the wrong
  answer look right: the reverse-coded items correlate at −0.56 with the rest,
  so an inference from correlation **would get all eight right here**.

  That is exactly why the app must not do it. The identical evidence appears when
  two subscales measure opposing constructs, and nothing in the numbers
  distinguishes the two cases. Reverse-coding needs the codebook, which the app
  does not have (`DOMAIN_PACKS.md` §07). A pack that guessed would be right on
  this file and confidently wrong on the next one — which is the failure mode the
  document exists to prevent, reproduced in miniature.

  **A drive that sees eight items silently flipped has found a bug, even though
  the flips are correct.**
- **No `melt_repeated`.** See the false alarm below.
- **No scale total computed and offered as a feature.** Scoring is a decision
  about the instrument, and summing reverse-coded items unflipped produces a
  number that means nothing.
- **No log transform, no Pareto scaling, no detection-limit reading.** Wrong
  lens.
- **No grain question answered for the user.** One row per respondent; the
  suggestion returns only `age`, which is shape noise, and questions 4–7 do not
  fire.

## False alarms this fixture reproduces on purpose

| Finding | Severity | Ledger | What should happen |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mixed_units__education` — *"'education' mixes different units"*, *"Found l, s in the same column"* | **critical** | **`IMPORT-267`** | Nothing. This is a live false positive and the fixture reproduces it deliberately. |
| `wide_repeated_measures` — *"1 group of columns look like repeated measures"*, proposing `melt_repeated` | info | — | Reframed under the survey lens: 40 items of one instrument, not one quantity measured 40 times. |
| `binary_text__sex` | warning | — | A correct reading. |

### `IMPORT-267`, because a driver will hit it first

`education` holds `High school`, `Some college`, `Bachelors`, `Graduate`. There
is no number in the column. `check_numeric_stored_as_text` reaches its
mixed-units branch anyway, and `_KNOWN_UNITS` contains the single letters `g`,
`m`, `l` and `s` with no requirement of a preceding digit — so the `l` of
*school* and the `s` of *Bachelors* are read as two different measurement units,
and the app asserts at **critical** severity that a column of education levels
mixes units.

This is the governing rule's own failure at the highest severity the app has.
`ml/import_doctor.py` is frozen (`TRANSITION_PLAN.md` §05), so it is filed and
not fixed. The class is wide: `{Nursing, Bachelors}`, `{Farm, Berlin}`,
`{Fasting, Random}` all trip it.

It is left in the fixture rather than designed around, because a fixture that
avoids the app's live defects tests an app that does not exist.
