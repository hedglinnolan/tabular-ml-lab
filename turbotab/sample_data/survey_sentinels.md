# `survey_sentinels.csv` — expected findings

**300 rows × 45 columns.** `survey_instrument.csv`'s exact shape — 40 five-point
Likert items, `respondent_id`, `age`, `sex`, `education`, `sought_support` —
with a codebook's missing codes written into five items.

Generated by `make_fixtures.py::survey_sentinels`, seed `5588` over `survey()`.

**Why it exists.** §B1.1 calls sentinel detection **"the highest-yield check in
this pack"**, and until L41 no fixture here carried one.
`survey_instrument.csv` is a clean return by construction, so a detector for
this shape could not be driven against anything — and the check the research
ranks first was, for that reason, unbuildable.

**Deliberately a second file rather than a change to the first.** The clean
instrument is the fixture eleven existing claims are written against, including
the reframing that proves the wide-shape false alarm is corrected under the
survey lens. Seeding sentinels into it would have moved every one of them.

---

## The codebook this stands for

The ordinary one: **7 = not applicable, 8 = don't know, 9 = refused**, on a 1–5
item. §B1.1's own sentence: *in a 1–5 item, a `9` is not "extremely agree," it
is "don't know" or "refused."*

| Item | Code | n | share | Why that item |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `item_14` | 9 | 33 | 11.0% | the sensitive question — a refusal rate is an item property, not a table property |
| `item_05` | 9 | 18 | 6.0% | **also reverse-coded** — the cell the audit has to get right |
| `item_22` | 8 | 24 | 8.0% | don't know |
| `item_33` | 8 | 15 | 5.0% | don't know |
| `item_09` | 7 | 12 | 4.0% | not applicable |

**102 sentinel values across five items.** Every other item stays 1–5, so the
block's observed support — taken as the union across all 40 — is exactly 1–5
and the sentinels are exactly what breaks it.

**`item_05` is the deliberate both-at-once cell.** It is reverse-coded *and*
carries refusals, so its item–rest correlation is meaningless until the 9s come
out: a 9 read as *extremely agree* on a reversed item pushes the correlation the
wrong way twice.

## Must surface

- `pack::survey::sentinel_codes` at **`critical`**, naming all five items, with
  the **mean shift** per item — the `[X]` §B1.1 leaves in its coaching sentence.
  Nothing is recoded.
- `pack::survey::ordinal_declared` — the block is still found, with all **40**
  items in it. Before L41 the five sentinel-carrying items dropped out of their
  own instrument, which is what made this check unreachable.
- The reverse-coding audit at `/project/{id}/evidence/reverse-coding`, with
  **102 sentinel values excluded before any correlation is computed** and the
  exclusion stated.

## Must NOT surface

- **Any recoding.** `DOMAIN_SCIENCE.md` §01.2's third hard stop: some
  legitimate scales do run 0–9, and no signature in the data separates a scale
  point from a missing code.
- **A proposed reversal.** §B1.2 is SETTLED that correlations cannot
  distinguish the four causes of a negative item–rest correlation. The eight
  genuinely reverse-worded items *are* the negative ones here, which is exactly
  why the app must not act on that.
- **A PASS or FAIL against 0.30.** It is Nunnally & Bernstein's convention.

## What this fixture cannot show

- **A 6 in a 1–5 block.** A 6 breaks the run and would be reported as a
  candidate code; whether it is a mis-keyed item or a codebook value is exactly
  what the app declines to decide. Deliberately absent so the file stands for a
  codebook rather than for a data-entry error.
- **A sentinel that does *not* break the run** — a 9 on a 0–9 NRS, which must
  never be flagged. No fixture here has a 0–9 instrument; covered by a
  constructed frame in the test file.
- **Item text.** This is a numeric export with no labels, so the audit's `text`
  column is empty on every row. §B1.2 specifies the column and a codebook
  upload is the obvious source; nothing here exercises it.
- **A negative `-8` / `-9` code.** The shift would be negative and the sign is
  carried, but every code here is above the run rather than below it.
