**This is the loop. `GUIDED-178`, specified by the product owner in conversation on 2026-08-07, and

### B0 · The mechanical fact this is blocked on, and it is the real deliverable

**`DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md` §05.2: *"Motion's job is to preserve identity across a state change, so the
user never loses track of what became what."* And: *"An object that is destroyed and replaced teaches
nothing, however smoothly it fades."***

**Measured at L47 and still true**: `turbotab/web/index.html` has **106** `innerHTML =` assignments,
22 node-owning writes, and **0** `startViewTransition`, **0** FLIP `getBoundingClientRect`, **0**
`.animate()`, **0** `transitionend`.

**A deck rebuilt by `innerHTML` on every state change is destroy-and-replace.** The user cannot
follow which card became which, which is the entire pedagogical claim of the design. So the deck's
first requirement is **node-owning writes for the deck region** — cards created once, retained,
mutated in place, reordered rather than reprinted.

**Build that for the deck region only.** Do not refactor the other 106. But **file the class**: the
door acquires surfaces and none acquires a mechanism, and the L47 table is the evidence.

### B1 · The correction to the product owner's framing, which makes it constitutional

He said the place transforms **when you scroll**. Rule 5 of §05 is **"Nothing else moves"** and
forbids ambient animation; a scroll-driven transform is hover theatrics at page scale.

**The face changes because the analysis advanced — not because you scrolled.** Scrolling takes you to
where that already happened. That keeps it cause-and-effect, which is what §05 is titled. **Tell him
you made this change and why**; it is his design and he gets to overrule it.

### B2 · What to build — face one, in full

**One card per model. The card is that model's dossier. The deck is the shelf, and its order is the
judgment** — which is `PRODUCT_VISION.md`'s *"the shelf is never shortened; judgment is rendered as
order, not as absence"* made physical. **No card is ever removed from the deck.**

**Face 1 · Preprocess — *what this model needs from your data, and why.*** Build this one.

- The before/after table for **that model's** pipeline.
- **The pedagogical payload, and it is the whole reason this beats a single table**: preprocessing is
  **not one pipeline**. A gradient-boosted tree does not need scaling; KNN needs it badly; a linear
  model needs it for coefficient comparability. Most tools hide this by applying one pipeline to
  everything. **The deck makes per-model preprocessing visible.**
- So each transform carries **the reason from the model's own geometry** — *"standardized: distances
  are meaningless across mixed units"* against *"left raw: splits are scale-invariant."* Source these
  from the existing recipe/pipeline record. **Quote the record; do not compose new prose.** L36 and
  L53-B both ruled on this.
- A model whose pipeline cannot be built **says what is missing**, in the vocabulary L52 built.
