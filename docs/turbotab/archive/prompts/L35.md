## 00 · What this work is, stated first because it determines how you write

TurboTab is **research software**. Your job is statistical methodology and software
engineering — routing logic, test design, figure specifications, reference tables. It is not
clinical practice, not patient care, and there is no patient anywhere in this system. The
"biology" is reference data and methodological literature: unit-conversion constants,
physiologic plausibility bounds, DRI tables, QC thresholds, reporting checklists like
TRIPOD+AI and STROBE-nut.

**Precision is the safety property and hedging is the defect.** The governing rule:

> The app may be **silent**, and it may **refuse**, but it must never **assert something false.**

There is already a calibrated apparatus for uncertainty: every advisory carries
SETTLED / CONVENTION / DISPUTED naming where the field stands, every claim carries a source
resolving to a research file and section, and `[verify-at-build]` numbers are structurally
forbidden from shipping as constants. A pre-commit gate enforces it. A second, uncalibrated
layer of caution on top does real damage — it makes a SETTLED fact and a DISPUTED one read
the same, which is the exact failure the badge prevents.

So say the specific thing. You must be able to write *"1 IU retinol = 0.3 µg RAE while
1 µg vitamin D = 40 IU, and conflating them is a 12× error"* and *"a systolic pressure below
30 mmHg is physiologically impossible in a living outpatient while 812 readings above 140 are
the sickest patients and must be kept."* Where you genuinely do not know, the honest move is
DISPUTED with both positions, or a `BLOCKED.md` entry. Never a vague gesture.

## 04 · What the adjudication found, because it sets your loop

Driving the app after L34 produced six findings. Read these rows in full before starting —
`GUIDED-089`, `GUIDED-090`, `GUIDED-091`, `GUIDED-092`, `GUIDED-093`, `GUIDED-094`,
`GUIDED-095`.

The headline is **`GUIDED-095`**, and it is the class:

> The app records **36 kinds of decision.** `training.py` reads **six** project attributes —
> `grain`, `lockbox`, `model_shelf`, `target`, `task_type`, `working_table` — and contains no
> reference to `recipes`, `model_recipes`, the missingness declarations,
> `deferred_transforms`, `selection_spec`, `engineered`, `purpose` or `lens`.

The dividing line is not *which* decision. It is **whether the decision executed immediately.**
Clause §06 splits preprocessing in two: row-local operations, which may run now, and stateful
ones, *recorded now and fitted inside each training fold* so the held-out rows never inform
them. The first class reaches the model because executing rewrites `working_table`. The second
class reaches nothing — the record is written, the receipt counts it, the sentence is composed,
and **the executor does not exist.**

**Be clear about what is and is not broken.** Fitting inside the fold *works* — the pipeline is
constructed inside the estimator and the seal probes assert bitwise equality of every fitted
parameter after moving the held-out rows. What is missing is **fidelity**: the fold fits the
app's defaults rather than the user's choices. The app is *safe* and *unfaithful*, which is the
harder failure to see, because every number it prints is honest about the seal and none of them
is about the analysis the user specified.

Measured, so it is concrete. `metabolomics_untargeted.csv`, column `bmi`, 8 blanks of 80. The
user answers the mechanism **informative** and picks **indicator** — the strategy whose whole
point is preserving that signal without inventing a value. Preprocess does its half correctly:
creates `bmi_was_missing`, leaves the 8 blanks, and composes *"A was-it-missing indicator is
added for `bmi`; the underlying value is left blank."* Then `_pipeline` puts `bmi` in the
numeric block and fills those 8 blanks with the median, **27.15**. And
`blocks('informative', 'impute_median')` is `True` — so a user who *chose* median here would
meet §07's blocker and carry it as a manuscript limitation. The user who chooses correctly gets
median imputation silently.

**Why this outranks finishing the journey.** In the product owner's framing: the steps are not
the product, the connective tissue between them is. Knowing that *a blank in `bmi` means
something* constrains what may be imputed, which constrains what may be fitted, which constrains
what the methods section may claim — that chain is what takes years to learn and what the app
exists to carry. This is the first place it has been measured severed, at the seam between the
app's most sophisticated reasoning and the only number the user takes away.

## Part B — the substantial build: the fitted pipeline is composed from the record

**`GUIDED-095`, and `GUIDED-089` closes with it.**

Build **one builder** that composes the fold-fitted pipeline from what the user recorded:
the missingness declarations, the recipe variants (`model_recipes`), and the deferred transforms.
`training._pipeline`'s hardcoded ColumnTransformer is replaced by it.

**You are not deciding what is stateful. Read it.** The classification already exists, twice,
correctly, with the litmus reasoning attached — this is `AUDIT-008`'s shape and the fix is a
call, not an implementation:

- `turbotab/features.py` — `ROW_LOCAL` / `STATEFUL` per transform, and `apply()` already refuses
  to materialize anything not `ROW_LOCAL`. Line ~186 is the stateful block, annotated *recorded
  now, fitted in-fold.*
- `turbotab/recipes.py` — `scope` per operation with `because` stating the litmus answer;
  `resolve(model_key, operation_key)` gives the per-model variant.
- `turbotab/missingness.py` — `strategy(key)` carries the timing; `STRATEGIES_BY_BRANCH` is the
  single table for what a branch permits.

**Two rules that are not negotiable.**

1. **The sentence and the pipeline are one object, not two that agree.** L34 already applied
   this discipline to the transcript / receipt / methods triple, asserted as *identity*. Same
   here: the methods sentence must be derived from the same spec that builds the pipeline. Two
   strings that happen to agree today are two strings, and this defect is exactly what happens
   when they drift.
2. **Where a declaration cannot be honored for a given model, the run says which and why.**
   `leave` on a linear model is the case — the card's own copy says gradient boosting handles a
   blank natively and linear and neural models do not. That is a *stated divergence*, never a
   silent substitution. The shelf is never shortened and neither is the plan.

**Order it hardest-first.** Start with `leave` on a model that cannot accept NaN, because it is
the case most likely to bend the abstraction — it is the one where per-model divergence is
forced and the recipe lattice has to carry it. If the abstraction survives that, the rest is
fill-out.

**The gate, and it is behavioral.** A user who answers **informative** and picks **indicator**
on `bmi` gets a fit in which those 8 blanks are **not filled** — asserted on the fitted
transformer, not on the payload — and the methods sentence matches what was fitted. Drive it;
do not verify it through the API alone.

**And delete the mitigation when it stops being true.** `training.py`'s note *"not the recorded
preprocessing plan"* and the probe
`test_the_seal_holds.py::test_the_run_records_what_it_did_not_use` both exist because the plan
was not applied. When it is, they change together — a test that asserts a defect is a
placeholder with a deadline, and this is the deadline.

## Part C — `GUIDED-093`, and the fixture rule behind it

**Critical, and Part B cannot be honestly verified on clinical data until it lands.**

One line: `result.metrics` is assigned, then `result.predictions = [float(v) for v in y_pred]`
raises on a string class label, and the handler labels the whole result a failure. Measured on
`sample_data/clinic_visits.csv`, target `outcome` (`responder` / `non-responder`): every model
carries **Accuracy 0.857 AND** `error="ValueError: could not convert string to float"` **AND**
zero predictions.

Three false statements come out of it. The third is the one that matters: the calibration figure
tells the researcher *"The models that were fitted do not produce probabilities, so there is
nothing to calibrate."* **About logistic regression.** That sentence lives inside the not-drawn
apparatus `GUIDED-065` built one loop ago to be precise about which of four states applies, and
it is stating a falsehood about the user's model class to explain the app's own serialization
bug.

**The fix is not only `float()`.** Predictions and probabilities must serialize a class label as
what it is, and **no result may carry a metric and an error at once** — if serialization failed,
the metric is not trustworthy either. One or the other, never both. The existing guard
`assert result["metrics"] or result["error"]` is satisfied when *both* are set; it has to assert
they are **mutually exclusive.**

**The fixture rule, which is the real lesson.** This survived two loops because every Train and
calibration claim uses `leaky_sepsis.csv`, whose target is 0/1, so `float()` succeeds. String
outcomes are the ordinary case in clinical research. This is `GUIDED-075` one level over: the
project learned *don't verify through the API, drive the page*; it has not learned the data
version, **don't verify against the fixture that works.**

So: every claim about a journey step must run against **at least two fixtures of different
target shape**, and must **name the shape it did not cover.** Apply that to the Train and
calibration claims in this part. File the rule as a finding so it binds beyond this loop.

## Part E — the probe: does a recorded decision change anything?

The instrument that would have caught `GUIDED-095` on the day it appeared, and will catch the
next severed seam without anyone thinking to look.

**For each of the 36 recorded decision kinds:** build two projects identical except for that
answer, and assert **something downstream differs** — the fitted pipeline, the run's metrics or
features, the findings, the figures, or the composed prose. A decision whose flip changes nothing
anywhere is severed connective tissue.

**Decisions that legitimately have no downstream effect go on an explicit allow-list with a
stated reason**, in the file, not in a comment. That list is the honest output of this probe:
a sweep that reports only what it fixed has not reported its coverage.

**Report three counts:** kinds probed, kinds that propagated, kinds allow-listed. If you cannot
construct a flip for a kind, say so and why — that is a fourth count, not a silent omission.

Expect this to find more than `GUIDED-095`. `purpose` and `lens` are absent from `training.py`
entirely, and `selection_spec` and `engineered` are too.

## The rules that will be checked when you report

- **`FIXED` requires a named regression test, verified to fail on revert** — and to fail *for
  the stated reason*. Roughly one in five first-attempt reverts produces a plausible false
  failure.
- **File the defect *class*, not only its instance.** This is the single most common gap in an
  otherwise good report. A class that lives only in prose in a report gets rediscovered.
- **Never move a threshold in the same loop as the change that pressured it.**
- **Return nothing rather than a wrong value.** `(None, None)`, not `(0.0, 1.0)` — those are the
  values of *perfect* calibration and returning them from ignorance asserts perfection.
- **Where a claim is about behavior, drive it.** `turbotab/pageharness.py` runs the page's real
  controller in node against responses captured from a `TestClient` drive, and
  `__harness.calls()` reports exactly which routes it fetched. Reserve grep for claims that are
  genuinely about the file.
- **Never run `git add -A`.** Stage explicit paths; check `git status` first, every time.
- **Ambiguity is `OPEN`, never `FIXED`.**
- If you are blocked or something looks structurally wrong, **stop and write it in
  `BLOCKED.md`** rather than working around it.
