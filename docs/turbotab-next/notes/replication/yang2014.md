# Yang 2014 (added sugar and CVD mortality): can the data be retrieved to replicate it in TurboTab?

Checked 2026-10-15. Each fact is tagged **[V]** (verified at the URL given, or in the file named) or **[U]** (unverified: inferred, from memory, or not stated in any source).

## Verdict

**Approximate only. An exact replication is not possible from public data.** The parts can be retrieved, but six differences remain, each named in section 5:

1. **Mortality file version.** The follow-up-through-2006 public LMF that the authors used (the "2010" release) is no longer posted. Only the 2019 public LMF is, so the data must be re-censored at 2006-12-31. For people who died, that can only be approximated.
2. **CVD definition.** The 2019 public LMF has only `UCOD_LEADING`, so the paper's I00–I78 definition cannot be built. Heart disease plus cerebrovascular disease is the nearest substitute; it misses I10, I12 and I70–I78.
3. **Linkage and perturbation.** The 2019 public LMF uses a newer linkage algorithm, and some decedents' follow-up time or cause of death was replaced with synthetic values.
4. **Added-sugar database.** The authors' mapping of NHANES III food codes to MPED 1.0 is not published. The NCI Pyramid Servings Database for NHANES III is the closest public substitute: it gives added sugar in teaspoons per 100 g for all 4,542 NHANES III food codes.
5. **Exposure model.** The headline hazard ratios come from the NCI usual-intake method with regression calibration inside a Cox model, evaluated at quintile mid-values. They are not from a Cox model on observed quintiles. TurboTab cannot fit this today.
6. **Unstated details.** The weights used, the time scale and the teaspoon-to-kcal factor are not stated in the paper.

The targets TurboTab can reach today, after some data preparation outside the app, are:
- **eTable 7:** sugar-sweetened beverages from the food-frequency questionnaire, observed categories, Cox.
- **eTable 3:** first-day recall, restricted cubic spline.
- A first-day version of **Table 3** (cuts at 10% and 25%).

With the 2022 restricted LMF in an NCHS Research Data Center (RDC), items 1 to 3 can be closed, except that the linkage itself differs from the 2006 one. **[U]**

## 2. What the authors used (from the Methods) [V unless marked]

All items in this section: [V: PMC XML body].

**Two analyses.**
1. **Trends.** NHANES III (1988–94), 1999–2004 and 2005–2010, total n = 31,147.
2. **Association.** The NHANES III Linked Mortality cohort, 1988–2006, n = 11,733.

**Cohort exclusions (NHANES III).** Starting from 16,274 nonpregnant adults aged 20 or older, they excluded:

| Reason | n removed |
|---|---|
| Incomplete first-day 24-hour recall | 588 |
| History of MI, stroke or CHF | 1,321 |
| Diagnosed diabetes or on diabetes medication | 996 |
| Cancer at baseline | 452 |
| BMI < 18.5 | 279 |
| Missing covariates | 905 |

That leaves **11,733**, and the arithmetic checks.

**Trend samples, with the same six exclusions in the same order.**

| Survey | Start | Removed | Analyzed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1999–2004 | 13,422 | 740, 1,560, 931, 757, 352, 296 | **8,786** |
| 2005–2010 | 16,089 | 826, 1,733, 1,358, 952, 265, 327 | **10,628** |

The arithmetic checks for both.

**Dietary data.**
- A first-day 24-hour recall was taken in person at the mobile examination center (MEC).
- In NHANES III, about 8% of adults had a second recall (n = 962, per the Discussion).
- In 1999–2002 there was no second recall. Second recalls, by telephone 3–10 days later, covered 93% (2003–04) and 88% (2005–10).
- Nutrients came from the USDA Food and Nutrient Database for Dietary Studies (FNDDS).
- Added sugar came from the MyPyramid Equivalents Database (MPED):
  - MPED **1.0** for NHANES III and 1999–2002;
  - MPED **2.0** for 2003–2010.
- For 2005–10, 410 of 5,440 food codes were missing from MPED 2.0 and were matched to the nearest code.
- **How NHANES III's codes were matched to MPED 1.0 is not described. [U]**

**Usual intake.**
- The NCI method (Tooze 2006/2010; Kipnis 2009) gave the usual percent of kcal from added sugar, used with regression calibration.
- Trend models: weekday vs weekend, age, sex, race/ethnicity.
- Association models add education, smoking, alcohol, antihypertensive medication, physical activity, family history of CVD and HEI-1995.
- They used a Box-Cox transformation with **λ = 2.5**. The value is stated in the text.

**Linked mortality.**
- NHANES III LMF linked to the National Death Index through 2006.
- Deaths from other causes were censored.
- **CVD = ICD-10 I00–I78 as the underlying cause.** In the 113-cause recode this is groups 054–074, under subtotal 053 "Major cardiovascular diseases (I00-I78)". [V: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/datalinkage/underlying-and-multiple-cause-of-death-codes-508.pdf]
- They cited the 2006-follow-up matching-methodology documents.
- **Whether they used the public-use or the restricted LMF is not stated. [U]**

**Covariate coding.**
- Race/ethnicity: 4 levels.
- Education: <12, 12, >12 years.
- Smoking: never, former, current.
- Alcohol: 0, <3, ≥3 drinks per week.
- Physical activity: 0, <5, ≥5 times per week of moderate-to-vigorous activity.
- BMI: <25, 25 to <30, ≥30.
- Family history of CVD and antihypertensive use: yes/no.
- Systolic blood pressure and total cholesterol: continuous.
- HEI-1995: 10 components, 0–100.
- **The NHANES III variable that implements each of these is not given. [U]**

**Adjustment models.**
1. Age, sex and race/ethnicity.
2. Fully adjusted: model 1 plus education, smoking, alcohol, antihypertensive medication, physical activity, family history of CVD, HEI, BMI, systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol and **total calories**.

**Exposure presentation.**
- *Quintiles (Table 2).* The 10th, 30th, 50th, 70th and 90th percentiles of usual intake were taken as quintile mid-values. Q2–Q5 were compared with Q1 from the continuous calibrated model; these are not dummy categories.
- *Cuts (Table 3).* Less than 10%, 10 to <25%, and 25% or more. Mid-values were 5.0%, 17.5% and 28.7%; 28.7% is the 95th percentile, used as a proxy.

**Other model details.**
- Proportional hazards was checked with Schoenfeld residuals.
- Interactions used the Satterthwaite F test.
- The number needed to harm (NNH) was taken at 15 years, with 500 rescaled bootstrap weights.
- Software: SAS 9.3 and SUDAAN 11.

**Weights and design.** SUDAAN was used "to take into account the complex sampling design" (paraphrase). **Which weight (presumably WTPFEX6 for the MEC sample) and which time scale (exam or interview origin, or attained age) are not stated. [U]**

### The questions asked about the LMF

- **Is the 2006 version still available?** No (FTP listing and 404s above). [V]
- **Re-censoring the 2019 file at 2006-12-31.** The public file has follow-up months from exam (`PERMTH_EXM`) but no death date. NHANES III's public files give the **month** of the MEC exam (`MXPTIMO`) but not its year; only the survey phase (1988–91 vs 1991–94, `SDPPHASE`). [V: exam.sas, adult.sas labels] So:
  - **Survivors** to 2019 can be re-censored exactly, because exam date = 2019-12 − `PERMTH_EXM`. [V by construction; the PERMTH definition is in the 2019 file description]
  - **Decedents** carry an uncertainty of up to about ±18 months in calendar time. A death within that window of 2006-12-31 cannot be placed on either side. It can be narrowed with the exam month, the phase, and the exam dates of surviving participants in the same pseudo-PSU. **[U] the proposed method and its precision.**
  - The 2019 linkage algorithm also changed. Vital status agreed for at least 98% of participants with the previous linkage over the same follow-up, so a small number of 2006 deaths will differ. [V: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/datalinkage/public-use-linked-mortality-file-description.pdf]
- **Does the perturbation affect cause-specific mortality?** Yes. For selected decedents, synthetic values replace follow-up time or underlying cause; vital status is unchanged. [V: same file description; the LMF landing page] For the first public NHANES III LMF (2000 follow-up), NCHS counted 1,188 heart-disease deaths in the public file against 1,158 in the restricted file, and 689 vs 698 cancer deaths. [V: https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/datalinkage/linked_mortality/archived_files/mort_2004_release/nh3_mort_compare_2007.pdf]
- **The CVD definition cannot be formed from the 2019 public file.** `UCOD_LEADING` offers only 001 (heart disease, 113-recode 054–068) and 005 (cerebrovascular, 070). [V: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/datalinkage/public-use-linked-mortality-files-data-dictionary.pdf]
  - Missing from I00–I78: 069 (I10, I12, essential and hypertensive renal), 071 (I70, atherosclerosis) and 072–074 (I71–I78, aortic aneurysm and other arterial disease). These deaths fall into 010, "all other causes". [V: the cause-of-death codes document]
  - The size of the loss is roughly 5–10% of CVD deaths. **[U] an estimate from memory of US mortality patterns.**
- **RDC.** The restricted LMF (UCOD_113, exact dates, multiple causes) is available only through an NCHS RDC. Public and restricted LMFs cannot be combined there. [V: LMF landing page https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/linked-data/mortality-files/ ; the old `data-linkage/mortality-public.htm` redirects there]

### The questions asked about added sugar

- **Does any MPED or FPED version exist for NHANES III's food codes?** No. MPED 1.0 covers 1994–2002 and FPED starts in 2005. [V: MPED 1.0 fact sheet https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400530/pdf/fped/MPED_1_Fact_Sheet.pdf; FPED overview]
  - NHANES III's 7-digit codes are the first 7 digits of the CSFII 1994–96 8-digit codes. [V: https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/pyramid/documentation/]
  - So MPED 1.0's `equiv9400.txt` can be matched on the first 7 digits for most foods, and the authors presumably did this. **[U]** The rest would need rules like the PSDB's.
- **Can added sugar be derived for NHANES III recalls from public data today?** Yes, through the PSDB. Keyed on `DRPFCODE` and `DRRFCODE`, it gives teaspoons of added sugar per 100 g for every food reported. [V]
  - Then % kcal = teaspoons × (grams of sugar per teaspoon) × 4 ÷ total kcal (`DRPNKCAL`) × 100.
  - MPED 2.0 sets 1 teaspoon equivalent = 4.2 g of sugar, and says the equivalent definitions are the same as MPED 1.0's. [V: mped2_doc.pdf]
  - The PSDB's own grams per teaspoon, and the kcal factor the authors used, are **[U]**. The paper does not state them.
- **How far off would the PSDB be?** It derives from the CSFII 1994-96 Pyramid Servings Database, which came before MPED 1.0. Added-sugar values for the same food may differ slightly from MPED 1.0. **[U] not quantified.**
  - A useful check: compare the weighted mean first-day % kcal from added sugar against the paper's figures. Table 1's 15.7% is a *usual-intake* mean. The first-day category cut-offs in eTable 2 and eTable 3 are another anchor.

## 5. Feasibility and what TurboTab would need

### Differences from the paper that cannot be removed with public data

1. **Mortality file.** The 2019 public LMF must be re-censored at 2006-12-31. Survivors can be done exactly; decedents only approximately. On top of that come the new linkage and the perturbation.
2. **CVD definition.** Heart disease plus cerebrovascular disease (`UCOD_LEADING` ∈ {001, 005}) replaces I00–I78. It misses I10, I12 and I70–I78.
3. **Added-sugar database.** PSDB teaspoons replace the authors' unpublished NHANES III → MPED 1.0 matching.
4. **Unstated details.** The teaspoon-to-kcal factor, the weight variable, the Cox time scale, and the coding of covariates (family history, physical activity, alcohol) are all **[U]**.
5. **Exposure model.** The NCI usual-intake model plus regression calibration in Cox (Box-Cox λ = 2.5) has to be rebuilt or skipped.

### What TurboTab already has [V: repo, read-only]

- An XPT v5/v8 reader: `turbotab/core/xport.py`. It covers the 1999–2010 files and NHANES III's few XPT files, such as the prescription-medicine file `pupremed.xpt` and the vitamin K files.
- Survey-weighted Cox: Binder pseudo-likelihood, Efron ties, Taylor linearization over strata and PSUs, with domains rather than deletions. See `turbotab/core/models/survey.py` (docstring and the `survey_cox` contract) and `turbotab/core/models/survival.py` (`cox_fit(weights=…)`).
- `PERMTH_INT`/`PERMTH_EXM` and `MORTSTAT` are recognized as follow-up time and event (`turbotab/core/estimand.py:111-130`).
- Exposure forms: quintiles, categories at declared cuts (10 and 25 can be entered), and a restricted cubic spline with 3–5 knots (`decisions.py` `SetExposureForm`; `methods/exposure_form.py`).
- Energy adjustment, including density plus energy (`methods/energy.py`).
- The NCI usual-intake *distribution* (amount-only and two-part models, a weekend indicator, BRR or PSU bootstrap) in `methods/usual_intake.py`, and regression calibration for least-squares and logistic models in `methods/calibration.py`.
- Design-based trends across stacked cycles: `methods/cycle_trends.py` and `methods/cycles.py`.

### Gaps

1. **Fixed-width ASCII with SAS INPUT layouts.** Ingest accepts CSV/TSV/TXT, Parquet, Excel and XPT only (`turbotab/core/datastore.py:3, 45-51`). Every NHANES III core file (`adult`, `exam`, `lab`, `examdr`, `iff`, `iffse`, `hei`) and both LMFs are `.dat` fixed-width files. The column positions sit in each file's `.sas` INPUT block, or in the R `fwf_cols` for the LMF. Needed: a `.dat` + `.sas` layout reader, or conversion to Parquet before ingest.
2. **Summing food records to a person-day total.** Joins can be one-to-one, one-to-many or many-to-one, but combining a file's rows to one per identifier is "deep assembly, planned for v2.x" (`turbotab/core/assembly.py` docstring). `AggregationMethod` is mean/first/last/change only, with **no sum** (`decisions.py:826`). The pipeline needed is: IFF rows → join the PSDB on the food code → grams/100 × `add_sug` → **sum by SEQN** (and by recall day) → % kcal against `DRPNKCAL`.
3. **Derived columns.** No derive or recode decision exists among `decisions.py`'s `Set*` classes. `SetEvent` names one level of a binary column (`decisions.py:896`). So these have to be computed before ingest:
   - CVD death = `MORTSTAT`=1 & `UCOD_LEADING`∈{001,005};
   - % kcal from added sugar;
   - the covariate recodes.
4. **Calendar-date censoring.** `SetFollowUp.horizon` censors at one time on the follow-up scale (`decisions.py:924-940`). Re-censoring at 2006-12-31 needs a per-row administrative censoring time, months from each person's exam to that date. It would also need the decedent exam-date approximation described in section 3.
5. **Regression calibration in Cox.** The calibration stage says plainly that a Cox fit is not corrected (`turbotab/core/stages/calibration.py:141`). It also corrects only a linear term; a spline or quintiles are withheld (`method_previews.py:734`). Usual intake has no person-level covariates, and λ is limited to [0, 1.5] where the paper used 2.5 (`methods/usual_intake.py`). So Table 2 and Table 3 as published, with HRs at quintile mid-values of calibrated usual intake, cannot be reproduced in the app.
6. **Spline knots and reference.** eTable 3 puts knots at the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles. TurboTab takes a knot count with Harrell's placement, which is 10/50/90 for 3 knots (`SetExposureForm.knots`/`knots_rule`; `exposure_form.py` docstring). eTable 3 also uses a non-first reference quintile (Q2). **[U]** whether TurboTab reports spline HRs at chosen values against a chosen reference.
7. **Trends.** `cycle_trends` gives unadjusted design-based means with no covariates (Issue 9 in its docstring), and defaults to `SDMVSTRA`/`SDMVPSU` (`cycles.py:305`). Table 1's estimates are covariate-adjusted *usual-intake* means with pairwise t tests. Stacking NHANES III (`SDPSTRA6`/`SDPPSU6`, `WTPFEX6`) with the continuous survey would need unique strata codes. **[U]** whether `cycles.py` handles this.
8. **SAS7BDAT.** No reader was found. The MPED and FPED intake files are SAS7BDAT inside `.exe` archives, but FPED's per-100 g tables also come as `.xls`.
9. **NNH at 15 years with 500 rescaled bootstrap weights.** Not checked. **[U]**

### Realistic replication tiers

- **Tier A (TurboTab now, after preparation outside the app):**
  - eTable 7 (FFQ beverage categories, survey-weighted Cox, 831 deaths over 4 categories);
  - a first-day version of Table 3 (cuts 10 and 25 on first-day % kcal from PSDB added sugar);
  - first-day quintiles as an approximation of eTable 3 (Harrell knots, not 5/50/95).

  Expect the HRs to move because of the CVD definition, the 2019 linkage and the perturbation. Compare against the 831 deaths and 163,039 person-years.
- **Tier B (needs gaps 1–4 closed):** the same targets built entirely inside TurboTab.
- **Tier C (needs gap 5 and ideally the RDC):** Table 2 and Table 3 as published.
