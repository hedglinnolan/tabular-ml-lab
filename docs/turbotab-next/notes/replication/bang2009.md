# Replication audit: Bang et al. 2009, ADA diabetes risk score (NHANES)

Prepared 2026-10-15. This audit only reads sources: no NHANES data file was downloaded (HEAD requests only, plus an 80-byte range read of two file headers). The repo was read, not modified.

**Tags.** **[V: key]** means verified at the source named by the key (listed in §9). **[I]** means my inference from verified numbers, with the reasoning given. **[U]** means unverified.

---

## 0. Verdict

**Approximate replication from public NHANES alone is feasible; exact replication cannot be guaranteed.**

- Every NHANES file the development (1999–2004) and internal validation (2005–2006) analyses need is public and online today. None of it is restricted to the RDC.
- The paper's final model is fully specified (Table 2), so the target is a fixed six-predictor logistic model. The points can be checked by hand.
- It cannot be called exact for these reasons:
  1. Several analytic choices are not stated: fasting-hours restriction, BP reading used, "borderline" diabetes, missing-value handling for continuous items, and the exact weights.
  2. Some files and documentation were revised after the analysis.
  3. The 1999–2004 and 2005–2006 family-history questions differ.
  4. Whether the published performance tables are weighted is not stated.
- The external ARIC/CHS validation is **not** replicable from public data. It is controlled-access through BioLINCC.

## 2. What the authors used (from Methods and Results; [V: PMC] unless tagged)

### 2.1 Design and cycles
- NHANES 1999–2004 (three cycles) for development, NHANES 2005–2006 for validation. ARIC and CHS baseline data were used for external validation. The Methods say: "We utilized de-identified data from multiple waves of NHANES (i.e., 1999–2006) that are publicly available."
- Inclusion: "aged ≥20 years and had fasting plasma glucose (FPG) results." Exclusion: "pregnant women."
- Analysis software: SAS 9.1, "survey procedures with options of strata, cluster and weight."

### 2.2 Outcome and groups
- **Undiagnosed diabetes:** FPG ≥ 7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL) without known diabetes.
- **Known diabetes:** "yes" to "Other than during pregnancy, have you ever been told by a doctor or health professional that you have diabetes or sugar diabetes?", **or** reported using insulin or other diabetic medications. These people were excluded from the model fit.
- Other groups: normal (FPG < 100 mg/dL) and impaired fasting glucose (100–125 mg/dL). The model contrasts undiagnosed diabetes against normal plus IFG together.
- **HbA1c** is used only in an ancillary analysis, with the endpoint HbA1c ≥ 6.5%.
- Non-fasting participants were not used for NHANES model development.

### 2.3 Predictors in the final model (Table 2 and its footnote, verbatim definitions)
- **Age:** <40 (ref), 40–49, 50–59, ≥60.
- **Sex:** male vs female.
- **Family history of diabetes:** yes/no.
- **History of hypertension:** "reported a history of hypertension, reported using prescribed medication for hypertension, had a systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg, or had a diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg."
- **Obesity** (BMI and waist combined; waist in inches):
  - extremely obese if BMI ≥ 40, or waist ≥ 50 (men) / ≥ 49 (women);
  - else obese if 30 ≤ BMI < 40, or 40 ≤ waist < 50 (men) / 35 ≤ waist < 49 (women);
  - else overweight if 25 ≤ BMI < 30, or 37 ≤ waist < 40 (men) / 31.5 ≤ waist < 35 (women);
  - else not overweight or obese.
- **Physically active:** "more active" in answer to "Compare your activity with others of the same age"; otherwise not active.
- **Missing values:** "If information was missing or unknown in categorical variables, we defined the condition as absent." The same section also says: "Due to small proportions of missing data, we used all non-missing observations available."

### 2.4 Model building
- Multiple logistic regression with undiagnosed diabetes as the endpoint, diagnosed cases excluded.
- The initial model held all Table 1 variables plus their interactions with age. Continuous variables were used first and categorized later.
- Backward elimination removed the largest p-value one at a time, with judgment applied:
  - income and insurance were dropped although significant;
  - physical activity was kept at p = 0.06.
- **Points:** "We created a weighted scoring system by rounding up all regression coefficients in the final model to the nearest integer (when strong monotonicity was observed, we broke the tie accordingly)."
- **[I] The rule that reproduces Table 2 exactly:** take the ceiling of |log OR|, keep the sign, then raise a tied ordered category by one.
  - 0.27→1, 1.12→2, 1.99→2→**3** (tie with "obese" = 2 broken), 2.09→3, −0.34→−1.
  - Ordinary rounding reproduces only 6 of the 10 points.
- **Score:** score = (40–49)·1 + (50–59)·2 + (≥60)·3 + male·1 + family history·1 + hypertension·1 + overweight·1 + obese·2 + extremely obese·3 − active·1. The range is −1 to 9. [V: PMC Table 4 footnote]
- **Cutpoint:** ≥ 5 counts as high risk. ≥ 4 is offered for pre-diabetes.

### 2.5 Validation
- **NHANES 2005–2006:** % high risk, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, Youden index, LR+, LR− and AUC of the score. The score is compared with:
  - the CDC, ADA and USPSTF guidelines;
  - ADA questionnaire I (Herman 1995) and II (Heikes 2008);
  - the Rotterdam model.
- **ARIC/CHS (N = 19,728):**
  - the model was refit (Appendix Table 1);
  - the score was evaluated;
  - family history was imputed in CHS with SAS PROC MI;
  - 521 non-fasting ARIC participants were classed by random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL.
- **Ancillary analyses (NHANES):**
  1. undiagnosed diabetes plus pre-diabetes vs normal;
  2. age < 45 vs ≥ 45;
  3. the HbA1c ≥ 6.5% endpoint.

### 2.6 Weights (not stated precisely)
- The paper says only that summary statistics "were weighted in fasting subsamples" (Table 1 footnote) and that survey procedures used strata, cluster and weight. It does not name the weight variable.
- **[I]** The NCHS-correct 6-year fasting weight is 2/3 × WTSAF4YR for 1999–2002 rows and 1/3 × WTSAF2YR for 2003–2004 rows. This is the tutorial's MEC6YR formula applied to the fasting weights.
  - The tutorial formula is [V: TUT].
  - The 2-year and 4-year fasting weights are [V: LAB10AM, L10AM_B, L10AM_C].
- For 2005–2006 alone the weight is WTSAF2YR [V: GLU_D].

## 4. What changed after 2009 (today's files vs what the authors had)

Dates are from each documentation page's "Last Revised" line and the NHANES data-listing pages' "Updated" column [V: lab listings], plus the server's Last-Modified headers [V: HEAD].

1. **Glucose, 2005–06 vs 2003–04 (instrument and lab change).**
   - In 2003–04, glucose was run on a Roche Cobas Mira at the University of Missouri-Columbia. In 2005–06 it was run on a Roche/Hitachi 911 at Fairview, University of Minnesota.
   - GLU_D now gives a crossover regression: `Y (Cobas Mira) = 0.9835*X (Hitachi 911)`, `Y (Hitachi 911) = 0.9815*X (Cobas Mira) + 3.5707`, "may be used to trend the glucose data."
   - The page's revision note (Last Revised **August 2016**) reads: "An additional regression equation was added to compare 2003-2004 glucose and insulin data to 2005-2006 glucose and insulin data." [V: GLU_D]
   - The paper mentions no glucose adjustment [V: PMC]. **[I]** The authors used raw LBXGLU. A replication should too. Applying the 2016 equation would lower 2005–06 FPG by about 1.65%, so 126 on the Hitachi 911 becomes about 123.9 Mira-equivalent, and some validation cases would be reclassified.
   - Whether any glucose crossover equation existed in the 2008–2009 documentation is **[U]**.
   - The data values in GLU_D.xpt appear unchanged since 2014 (Last-Modified 22 May 2014, earlier than the 2016 documentation revision) **[I]**.
2. **Fasting glucose files 1999–2002 re-posted.**
   - LAB10AM: documentation "Last Revised: September 2009". L10AM_B: "Last Revised: March 2007".
   - Both XPT files carry server Last-Modified **10 Mar 2016**, while the other files carry 22 May 2014 (a site migration). Whether the 2016 re-post changed any values is **[U]**.
   - What the September 2009 LAB10AM revision changed is not stated on the page **[U]**. The paper was published December 2009 and cites a URL accessed 31 August 2009 [V: PMC ref 25], so the authors may have used the pre-September-2009 LAB10AM **[U]**.
   - L10AM_C: documentation revised August 2016; the XPT is dated 2014.
3. **Diabetes pills, 2005–06.**
   - DIQ070 became **DID070**. It was asked of people reporting diabetes *and* of those reporting pre-diabetes (DIQ010 = 3). Missing values for 103 people reporting pre-diabetes were imputed from the prescription-medication file. In 1999–2004 the question was asked only of people reporting diabetes.
   - DIQ_D is "Last Revised: April 2011", after the paper. Whether the imputation arrived in that revision is **[U]**.
   - Under the paper's definition (yes to DIQ010, or insulin, or pills), a "borderline" pill-taker counts as known diabetes in 2005–06 but cannot be identified in 1999–2004. [V: DIQ_D]
4. **Family-history wording differs by cycle.**
   - MCQ250A (1999–2004) includes grandparents; MCQ300C (2005–06) covers parents and siblings only, and MCQ250A/MCQ260 were discontinued. [V: MCQ, MCQ_D]
   - The paper notes that 2005–06 "collected the data on family history of diabetes but not separately for parents and sibling." [V: PMC Table 4 footnote]
   - **[I]** The authors' development family history is MCQ250A (any blood relative). Table 1 shows 46% with a family history among normal-glucose participants, against 20% with an affected parent and 7% with an affected sibling, which only a definition including grandparents explains.
5. **HbA1c (ancillary analysis only).**
   - The 2003–04 → 2005–06 crossover regression for A1c that GHB_D once recommended was **withdrawn in November 2011** as an over-correction. All four A1c files say "Last Revised: March 2012."
   - The data values themselves were not changed; the advice is to use the original data. Whether the authors applied the crossover in 2009 is **[U]**. [V: LAB10, L10_B, L10_C, GHB_D]
6. **DEMO files** were revised in September 2009 to add variables (education DMDEDUC3, month of exam, household reference person). The core variables used here are unaffected **[I]**. [V: DEMO, DEMO_D]
7. **Insulin** was method-changed in 2003 and 2005. It is not used by this paper.

## 6. Choices the paper leaves open (a replication must decide and record them)

1. **Fasting restriction.** Options: keep rows with WTSAF > 0 (fasted 8–24 h, per NCHS), or any non-missing LBXGLU. The documentation says LBXGLU can exist with a zero fasting weight. Suggested: WTSAF > 0.
2. **BP readings.** The mean of the available BPXSY1–4 / BPXDI1–4, or another rule, is not stated. Suggested: the mean of the available readings, with a sensitivity check on the first reading.
3. **DIQ010 = 3 ("borderline").** Known or not known is not stated. Suggested: not known (only 1 = yes), with a sensitivity check.
4. **"Prescribed medication for hypertension"**: BPQ050A (now taking) or BPQ040A (told to take). Suggested: BPQ050A = 1, with BPQ040A as a sensitivity check.
5. **Missing obesity inputs** (both BMI and waist missing) and missing glucose among people not known to have diabetes. Complete case vs "absent" is unclear.
6. **Family history in development.** MCQ250A (any relative) as inferred above, against MCQ300C in validation.
7. **Weights.** The SAS 9.1 analysis likely subset the rows rather than using a domain statement **[U]** (this is from memory of SAS feature history, unverified). That affects standard errors only, not ORs.
8. **Glucose calibration.** None was applied (raw LBXGLU).
9. **Backward-elimination path.** It involved judgment calls, so it is not reproducible. Fit the published final specification directly.

## 8. What TurboTab has, and what it lacks (repo read-only at branch turbotab-next, e6e58809)

### Present
- **XPT reader** `turbotab/core/xport.py`: SAS XPORT v5 and v8, streamed, IBM float conversion, SAS special missing values. NHANES 1999–2006 files are v5 ✓.
- **Ingest** `turbotab/core/datastore.py` (`_ingest_xpt`) and **SEQN joins** `turbotab/core/assembly.py` (one-to-one, one-to-many and many-to-one; many-to-many refused).
- **Cycle stacking** `turbotab/core/methods/cycles.py`:
  - SDDSRVYR codes 1–4;
  - the 1999–2002 four-year weight doubled and divided by the number of cycles, including **WTSAF4YR** for the fasting subsample (giving 2/3·WTSAF4YR and 1/3·WTSAF2YR);
  - strata and PSUs recoded per cycle;
  - declared `renames` (DIQ070→DID070, LBXGLUSI→LBDGLUSI) and `incompatible` declarations (glucose instrument, MCQ250A vs MCQ300C).
  - Its docstring says "**engine only until C1 asks it**", so it is not reachable from the app flow.
- **Weight recognizer** `turbotab/core/recognizers.py`:
  - recognizes WTSAF2YR, WTSAF4YR and WTSAF6YR/SAF6YR as the fasting subsample;
  - applies the NCHS least-common-denominator rule;
  - `FASTING_ANALYTES` includes LBXGLU and LBDGLUSI;
  - `subsample_variables` detects fasting-only columns from their values.
- **Design-based logistic regression** `turbotab/core/models/survey.py` (`weighted_logistic`, `survey_table`): Taylor linearization, domain (subpopulation) estimation, df = PSUs − strata. This reproduces SURVEYLOGISTIC-style ORs and CIs.
- **Categories at declared cut points** for a predictor's form: `turbotab/core/methods/exposure_form.py`. Whether this applies to covariates other than the exposure is [U].
- **Backward elimination by Wald p-value**: `variable_selection.backward_wald`. It is model-based, pooled over imputations, and not design-based. It is not needed if the published specification is fitted directly.
- **Internal–external validation by a cluster column**: `turbotab/core/methods/site_validation.py`, `models/validation.py`. With SDDSRVYR as the column, one fold is the paper's split (train 1999–2004, test 2005–06).
- **Sensitivity and specificity at risk thresholds, net benefit, Youden**: `turbotab/core/models/decision_curve.py`, on CV folds.

### Gaps
1. **Derived variables across columns.** The paper needs about eight row-level constructions:
   - the outcome from LBXGLU, DIQ010, DIQ050 and DIQ070/DID070;
   - the known-diabetes exclusion;
   - the pregnancy exclusion (RIDEXPRG);
   - hypertension (BPQ020, BPQ050A, mean SBP/DBP);
   - sex-specific BMI-or-waist obesity in inches;
   - active from PAQ520 = 1;
   - family history from MCQ250A/MCQ300C;
   - age bands.

   The only in-app derived column found is the log-scale outcome (`structural.derived_columns`). Without a derivation step, the analysis table must be built outside the app.
2. **Points-score derivation.** Nothing found that turns coefficients into integer points (rounding or ceiling, monotone tie-break) or reports the score's distribution.
3. **Scoring a fixed, published rule** (no refit) on new rows. This means AUC of an integer score, and % high risk, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, LR+, LR− and Youden at integer cutpoints (≥ 3 … ≥ 6). Needed for Tables 3 and 4 and for the six comparator rules. Not found.
4. **A declared holdout by cycle.** The holdout is a random fraction from 0.1 to 0.4 (`decisions.py`). The nearest alternative is internal–external validation by SDDSRVYR, which also runs and pools the other folds and evaluates a refit model rather than the frozen score.
5. **Cycle stacking is not wired into the app** (engine only).
6. **The "unknown counts as absent" convention** ("if you don't know the answer, enter 0") must be an explicit recode. The default missing-data handling (imputation or complete case) would differ.
7. **Minor:** `FASTING_ANALYTES` lacks the 1999–2004 SI name **LBXGLUSI**. The value-based `subsample_variables` should still catch it.
8. **Weighted vs unweighted performance.** The published tables appear unweighted (§5.3). TurboTab should be able to show both and label which one matches the paper.

### Suggested first step for a replication run (not done here)
Download the nine model files per cycle: DEMO, the glucose file, the A1c file, DIQ, BPQ, BPX, BMX, PAQ and MCQ. That is about 105 MB by the HEAD content-lengths, MCQ about 44 MB of it; all 61 XPTs come to 130 MB. Then rebuild the 3,396 / 1,652 / 210 / 482 counts and N = 1,640. That separates data drift from analytic choices before any model is fitted. This is a light job, not a heavy one.
