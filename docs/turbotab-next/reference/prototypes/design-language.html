Tabular ML Lab · Design language v1

The document is the interface.

A predictive-modeling analysis, rendered as a living interview: the app asks, the user answers, and the transcript that accumulates is the manuscript in embryo. This page is both the specification and the proof — the demo below runs on the same rules the spec writes down.

01Principles

Five commitments, ordered. When two collide, the earlier one wins.

Never assert falsely.

Inherited from the codebase's governing rule. The UI may be silent or may refuse, but every rendered statement traces to a recorded decision or a computed fact. Confidence below "high" never pre-selects.

The transcript is the artifact.

Answered questions collapse into prose sentences. Scrolling up is reading your methods section backward. Export is a formalization of what's already on screen, never a surprise.

Push the notable, pull the rest.

The app opens each section already oriented: ranked findings, ranked recommendations. The user is never asked "what do you want to see?" — they're shown what matters and offered more.

The past is editable, never silently destroyed.

Changing an earlier answer marks downstream work stale — visible, veiled, recoverable — and recomputation is an explicit, observable job. Nothing vanishes behind the user's back.

Only consequences move.

Motion exists to show cause and effect: sections settle when answered, arrive when opened, propagate when invalidated. No ambient animation, ever.

02The feed, live

A working slice of the interview: two recorded decisions, an open EDA section, the next branch, and the step after it waiting. Everything is operable — change a past answer, defer a finding to the ledger, mark evidence for the manuscript, answer the feature-engineering question.

    06Motion: cause and effect only

    1. Settle. An answered question collapses into its decision sentence in 250ms ease-out. The collapse is the visual receipt that the choice was recorded.
    2. Arrive. New sections grow downward from the point of consequence (300ms, 6px rise). The viewport never auto-scrolls; the user's scroll position is theirs.
    3. Propagate. Staleness sweeps downstream in document order, fast (≤150ms per section), so the user watches their edit's blast radius draw itself.
    4. Work is visible. Anything over ~1 second shows a job chip immediately — named in plain language ("Recomputing exploration…"), never a bare spinner.
    5. Nothing else moves. No ambient animation, no hover theatrics beyond elevation, and prefers-reduced-motion collapses everything to instant state changes.

    07Voice: how the app talks

    1. Questions are single and concrete. One decision per question block, named values, no compound asks. "Add the interaction age × creatinine?" — never "Configure feature engineering."
    2. Decisions are past-tense and exact. "184 rows were sealed" — a count, a verb, no adjectives. If the sentence couldn't appear in a methods section, it isn't a decision sentence.
    3. The coach observes; it does not command. Noticings state the evidence, the consequence, and the option — then wait. Amber text never says "you should"; it says "this usually means."
    4. Uncertainty is stated as uncertainty. Probe results say "on training folds only"; suggestions carry their basis. The UI never borrows confidence the math doesn't have.
    5. The app never speaks in the user's name. Analysis choices are recorded as the app's observations of the user's decisions; interpretive claims are left as authored gaps — the manuscript's [AUTHOR REQUIRED] rule, applied to the interface.
